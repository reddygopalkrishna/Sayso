Voice for the Mac, early prototype

Say it. Sayso does it.

Hold a key and speak: Sayso types clean text. Say a command: it opens apps and sites, clicks buttons by name, opens “the first result” and fills in forms from your saved details. No keyboard, no mouse.

Not available to download yet. Today it runs from source on a Mac.

What it does today

Each card says how far it has been tested, and what is still being tested is listed underneath. Nothing here is a promise about accuracy: that has not been measured on real recordings yet.

Type

Hold a key (Fn, or Right Option, Command or Control), speak, let go: clean text appears in the field you are in, in any app. Fillers like “um” are removed, lists and web addresses are formatted, and you can pick a Default or a Chill style.

Tried by hand.

Act

“Open Slack”, “new tab”, “close tab”, “go back”, “scroll down”, “click Sign in”, “open the first result”. Say “send” after a short pause and it presses Enter.

Tried by hand.

Say it your own way

When what you say is not an exact command (“log me in”, “open a fresh tab and search for AWS”), Sayso reads the names of the buttons on screen and works out the steps. It still asks before anything risky.

Tried against the live AI on sample sentences; not measured on real voice yet.

Fill in forms

Save your name, email and phone once in About me. Say “fill in my details” and Sayso puts each one in the right field. The AI only picks which fact goes where; it never sees the values.

Tried on a real web form through the app’s helper; not yet by voice.

Indian names and places

Built-in spellings for what speech recognition gets wrong: Hyderabad, Aadhaar, UPI, PhonePe, Zomato and common first names, plus your own word list.

Tested in code; real speech not measured yet.

Safe by default

A preview shows before an action runs, and Esc cancels it. Risky buttons (pay, delete) ask “yes?” first. Sayso never types into password fields and does nothing while a meeting app is in front.

The preview and the password-field check were tried by hand; “yes?” is tested in code.

Still being tested

Hands-free mode (it keeps listening and acts on “type …”, “send it”, “open …”, and on any command after the wake word “Sayso”) and messaging in Slack and WhatsApp are built and tested in code, but not yet confirmed end to end by a person speaking. Dictating in Telugu and other languages can be switched on in Settings, but has not been tried yet.

How it works

  1. Hear

    Speech is turned into text on your Mac with a Whisper model. Audio does not leave the computer.

  2. Decide

    Exact commands are matched instantly, with no AI. Anything else is typed as text, or, if you allow it, sent to an AI to work out the steps.

  3. Check

    Every action goes through the same safety checks: a preview, “yes?” for risky buttons, and no password fields or meeting apps.

  4. Do

    A small helper presses keys, clicks buttons and types text through macOS accessibility, so it works in the apps you already use.

English is the focus today. Telugu and Hindi are next.

Privacy

  • Your voice stays on your Mac. Speech recognition runs on the device.
  • Text goes to an AI only when it helps. Short, clean text is fixed locally. Longer text is tidied by an AI service, sending only the text and the names you have added to your word list.
  • Your own words feature is optional. It sends what you said, the app’s name and the names of buttons and links on screen. Never page text or what you typed, and never from a password field or a meeting app. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • Your details stay here. About me values are never sent anywhere, not even to the AI.
  • You control history. It is stored on your Mac, and you choose how many items to keep (or none).
